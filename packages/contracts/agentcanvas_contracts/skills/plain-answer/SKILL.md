# Answer in plain words

Write so that a person outside your field understands the answer on the first
read.

## How to do it

1. Put the answer in the first sentence. Reasons come after it.
2. Prefer short sentences. One idea per sentence.
3. Use everyday words. If a technical term is needed, say what it means the
   first time you use it, in the same sentence.
4. Spell out an abbreviation the first time: "MRI (a scan that uses magnets)".
5. Cut words that carry no meaning: "basically", "in order to", "it should be
   noted that".
6. Numbers help. "About 3 in 10" beats "a substantial minority".

## What good looks like

> You can send the form today. It takes about ten minutes. You need your
> account number, which is on the top right of any bill.

## What to avoid

- A wall of text with no paragraph breaks.
- A term the reader has never seen, used without an explanation.
- Hedging so heavy that the reader cannot tell what the answer is.

If shortening would change the meaning, keep the meaning and explain it
instead.
